# S03 — The Kettle & Coal

The Kettle & Coal is warmer than the street, and the street is warm enough. Its low ceiling is black with years of smoke from the hearth, and the air smells of wet wool, pine resin and frying onions. Caravan hands crowd the benches near the fire. Someone at the back is singing badly, and three others are telling him so, loudly and with great affection.

Pell, the pot-boy, weaves between the tables with a tray of mugs held high. He spots Ves at the door and nearly drops the lot. She pretends not to notice him going red.

Nothing much has changed in the room. The same faded map of the pass hangs over the hearth, with the same brown stain across the summit. A caravan hand laughs too loudly at his own joke and then checks whether anyone else heard it. Ves finds herself grinning at the stain before she can stop, and she is glad nobody is watching her do it.

At 20:00 on Day 1, Ves takes the corner table at the Kettle & Coal.

The corner table is the one she always took as a girl, under the shelf with the copper pans. It is still there, and so is the scratch in the wood where she carved her initials and was made to scrub them out. She sits with her back to the wall. Old habits are the first thing the road teaches a scout, and the last thing it lets go.

Mother Brisk comes out of the kitchen with her sleeves rolled to the elbow and sees Ves before Ves sees her. Her face does something complicated and then settles into a grin with a hard edge on it. "Look what the caravan dragged in," she says, and then she is holding Ves by both shoulders and looking at her as though checking for damage.

Mother Brisk pours Ves a cup of pine tea and takes her coin without a word.

They talk while the tea steams. Brisk tells her about the old mine below the gorge, sealed since the Night of Ash, and about the men who have started going up the road at night to look at it. She tells her about debts that were paid off in one year and borrowed again in the next. Then she lowers her voice. "The Lamplighter's been strange," she says. "Won't look at you when he talks."

Ves listens with her elbows on the table. She has heard Brisk talk like this before, always in a low voice and always with the kettle hissing behind her, and it has always meant that someone in Kettlehold was taking more than their share. She waits for the name. It does not come, and that is worse.

Ves asks to see the old salt cellar under the kitchen, and the answer comes back fast. “Not tonight,” Brisk says.
“Not with ash in the wind.”

The lamps on Kiln Street go dark at midnight, every night, without fail. Everyone in the room knows it, and nobody seems to think it strange.

Brisk goes back to the kitchen before Ves can ask again. Through the window the lamps along the street are lit one at a time, each a small yellow dot climbing the hill. A cart rattles past outside with no one walking beside it.

Ves hears the kitchen door swing twice and then stop. Somewhere behind it Brisk is talking to someone in a voice too low to carry, and the someone does not answer. Pell comes out with a fresh pot of tea, sets it down without a word, and goes back in again as quickly as he came.

Ves takes her tea to the window and stands there with the cup going cold in her hands. Outside, the town is settling. She watches the light on Saltgate's wet cobbles until she can no longer tell it from the reflection of her own face.

Ves listens to the town settle around her. The lamps on Kiln Street go dark at midnight, every night, without fail.
