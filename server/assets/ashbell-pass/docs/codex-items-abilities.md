# Items and Abilities

It is recorded that the Order keeps three items and one ability under this heading, and that none of them is quite what the town believes. The archive lists each one by its weight, its origin and the doors or heat it answers to. Where a rumour and a record disagree, the record is kept and the rumour is noted in the margin.

## The Bronze Clapper

The Bronze Clapper weighs as much as a grown goat and hangs from a chain of black iron. It strikes the inside of the bell, and it is the only part of the bell that can be lifted without the Bellwarden's leave.

The chain is black iron, riveted in nine links that the smiths renew every winter. A link that cracks in the cold is replaced at once, because a clapper that swings loose can strike the wrong side of the bell and sound a note nobody wants to hear. The Order keeps the spare links in a locked box in the tower.

The clapper was forged in Kettlehold, in the steam sheds below Kiln Street, and its founder's mark is worn smooth on one side. Smiths who have handled it say the iron rings true even when cold. Players who try to lift it alone will find that it needs two hands and a good rope.

Reports from the Bell Gate describe the clapper's sound as flat and short. The archivist attributes this to a crack in the lip of the bowl rather than to the metal itself.

## The Cinder Key

The Cinder Key is a *warm* iron key that never cools, even in snow. Its bow is shaped like a closed flame, and its teeth are cut for a lock that only the Order has ever made.

The Order forged the Cinder Key in the Abbey's lower forge, and the key has never cooled since. Archivists have tested it in ice water and in a snowdrift. The metal stayed warm in both, and the record calls this the key's only certain property.

The Cinder Key opens any door sealed by the Order of the Quiet Flame. Sealed doors carry a small ring of soot around the lock, and a key that feels warm against the ring is the one that works. Keys that do not work give no sign at all, which the archivist considers a design flaw.

Archivists list three things the key does not do. It does not open ordinary doors, it does not cool in a forge, and it does not work for anyone who has broken an oath to the Order. The last point is disputed, and the disputes are kept in a separate file.

A carrier who keeps the key close to the skin will feel it as a steady warmth. Carriers should set it down before entering deep snow, because a key held in a gloved hand is easy to lose in drifts. Every archivist who has signed the key's register has written the same note in the margin: keep it dry, keep it close, and never leave it on a sill.

## Ashsight

Ashsight is granted by the Ember Well, and only those who drink from the spring can carry it. The Ember Well lies in the Abbey's lower cloister, where the warm water rises through the stone.

Ashsight lets its bearer see the heat left by footsteps, for about a day. The trails glow faintly on stone and dust, and they cool in the order that the walker passed.

Ashsight is one of the few abilities the Order records as a gift rather than a skill. A bearer cannot train it, study it or buy it; the spring either grants it or does not. The archive lists four cases in which it was granted to a person who then could not see the trail at all, and it marks each case as unresolved.

Ashsight fades if its bearer sleeps before the trail is followed. Gameplay notes: the fade shows as a dimming of the heat overlay, and the interface should warn the player before it happens. A short fade warning is enough; a full pop-up breaks the trail. Designers should decide whether a sleep ends the trail or only dims it, and should write that choice into the quest log.

UI hint: show a small ember icon beside the trail counter, and let it pulse when the bearer is tired. The icon should never block the view of the trail itself. Keep the trail colour distinct from the warm glow of the Cinder Key, so a player never mistakes one for the other.

## Notes for Writers

Write the clapper and the key in plain terms, never as magic. The Order's archive calls them tools, and the people of Kettlehold call them heirlooms. Use the word tool when a character is talking to an archivist, and heirloom when a character is talking to a grandmother. Always capitalise Ashsight, because the interface uses it as a name.
