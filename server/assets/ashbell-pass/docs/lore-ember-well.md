# The Ember Well and Ashsight

The Ember Well is a hot spring in the lower cloister of Stillfire Abbey. Its basin is cut into the rock beneath a low arch, and the water rises warm and faintly bitter at all hours of the year. Steam hangs over the surface even in the coldest weather. The monks say the well has never frozen, and the Order's records do not contradict them.

## The rite of the well

The Order keeps the rite of the Well with great care. The Ember Well grants Ashsight only to those who drink from it before dawn. The water must be taken in a cup of plain clay, and the drinker kneels at the rim while the lower cloister is still dark. No lamp is lit in the cloister during the rite, and no one speaks until the cup is set down.

The rite is simple, and the Order does not allow it to be made more elaborate. A drinker who asks for chants, bells or incense is told that the well does not need them. A drinker who asks for a second cup is told that one is enough.

## What Ashsight does

Ashsight lets its bearer see the heat left behind by footsteps for about a day. The heat shows as faint red traces on stone, mud and cloth, and it fades as the day wears on. A tracker with Ashsight can follow a person who walked a road the day before, but a door that was shut or a stone that was thrown leaves nothing behind.

Those who have it describe the world as a map drawn in embers. Old footprints glow dull red on the steps, while fresh ones burn bright and leave a thread of warmth in the air. Water erases traces at once, so the Order advises its bearers to walk the dry stones and to keep away from the streams.

## Who may draw water

Only the Order of the Quiet Flame may draw water from the Ember Well. Outsiders who ask are sent back to the Abbey gate, where the door-keeper hears the request and says no. The Order does not sell the water, and it does not give it to people who have already been refused.

The Order's rule is strict because the water is not endless. The basin refills slowly from the rock, and the Order measures each cup against the level it keeps on a stone marker at the edge of the arch.

## Those who came too late

The Order's records describe several seekers who climbed the steps after the grey light had reached the cloister. They knelt at the rim and drank, and they saw nothing at all. Some left the Abbey in silence. Others stayed on for a season and tried again, and none of them reported what the Order's oldest texts promise to those who reach the well in time.

The records do not blame the seekers. They say only that the well keeps its own timing, and that the grey light is not a suggestion.

Scholars of the Order keep a separate ledger for the well. It lists the names of those who drank, the grey light they were given, and the hour of the first trail they reported. The ledger is short, and most of its pages are blank. The archivists say this is the most honest record in the Abbey, because it shows how rarely the well answers the question that is asked of it.

## Gameplay

In play, Ashsight works as a tracking mode. The player sees fresh heat trails on the map for about one in-game day after the drink, and older trails fade from the screen. Trails do not show through closed doors, and they tell the player where someone walked, not who they were.
