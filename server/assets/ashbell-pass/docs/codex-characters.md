# Characters

It is recorded that this codex lists the six people whose names the Order's archive mentions most often in connection with the Pass. Each entry sets out how the person looks, what they want, where they fall short, how they sound when they speak, and how they stand with the others. Where a passage is marked as history, it is set down as it was told to the archive, not as it is seen from the street.

## Ves Calloway

Ves Calloway is a caravan scout for the Red Mule Caravan, coming home to Kettlehold after six years away. She is twenty-four. She is lean and sun-browned from the high roads, and she walks with the careful balance of someone who has learned to keep her feet on wet rock. She carries a scout's pack, a short blade and a flint that she has never once let go cold.

Ves is stubborn, observant and quiet. Her want is simple to state and hard to meet: she wants to understand why the town she left is no longer the town she remembers. She is not sentimental about Kettlehold, but she is loyal to the people in it, and that loyalty makes her slow to accept an easy answer.

Her flaw is that she trusts what she has seen with her own eyes over what she is told. It is recorded that this has kept her alive on the pass and has also cost her friendships. She listens longer than most people expect, and she often says less than she knows.

Voice notes for casting: low, dry and unhurried. Ves speaks in short sentences and rarely raises her voice. When she is moved she goes quieter, not louder. Her humour is deadpan. Avoid a warm, bright tone, because she sounds like someone who has been awake for two days.

History: Corin taught Ves the bell and the bridge when she was a child, and she left the town without saying goodbye to him. She has ridden with the Red Mule Caravan for most of the years since. Ysolde Marr trusts her judgement on the road more than she trusts her own, which Ves finds flattering and a little worrying.

## Corin Ashdown

Corin Ashdown is the Bellwarden of Kettlehold and the keeper of the Ashbell. He is in his forties, broad in the shoulder and grey at the temples. He wears the Bellwarden's dark green coat buttoned to the throat, and he stands very straight when he is being watched, which is most of the time.

Corin is proud and dutiful. His want is for the town to keep its one bell and its one rule, so that nobody else has to pay the price for them. He would rather carry a cost alone than ask for help, and that habit is his flaw. It is recorded that he has mistaken silence for strength more than once.

Voice notes for casting: measured, formal and slightly clipped. Corin speaks for the town with the word "we" and for himself with the word "I" only when he must. He does not joke in public. In private he lets a little warmth through, and the difference is the point of his scenes.

History: Corin was Ves's mentor before she left, and he still speaks of her as the best tracker he ever trained. He worked beside Ysolde for years as her sworn partner, and the town assumed the two would grow old together at the Bell Gate.

## Ysolde Marr

Ysolde Marr is the captain of the Red Mule Caravan and has never lost a mule to the pass. She is tall, with a red scarf knotted at her throat and rings on three fingers. She laughs easily and looks at people as if she has already priced them.

Ysolde is charming and deep in debt. Her want is to keep the caravan running and to clear what she owes before the debts clear her. Her flaw is that her charm is also her way of avoiding a plain answer. Ysolde smuggles ember salt down the pass, and the caravan's ledgers show the lamp oil and salt going up the road while the ember salt goes down it, quietly.

Voice notes for casting: warm, quick and teasing, with the slang of the caravan road in her speech. Ysolde lapses into a lower, flatter voice when she is working a deal. She never raises her voice when she lies, and that is the tell to listen for.

History: Ysolde and Corin were sworn partners until 18:00 on Day 2, when Ysolde broke the oath in front of the Bell Gate. The archive records the oath as it was told afterwards, by people who were watching from the square. Ysolde has not offered her own account, and the town still speaks of the broken oath as the moment the Pass began to change.

## Abbess Hesper Quill

Abbess Hesper Quill is ninety years old and has not left Stillfire Abbey since the Night of Ash. She is small and very upright, wrapped in grey wool even in summer. Her eyes are pale and watch the door before they watch the visitor.

Hesper wants the Order's seals to hold and the Ember Well to remain in careful hands. Her flaw is that she decides what others may know, and she keeps secrets for their own good. It is recorded that she has never once admitted to being wrong, though the archive suspects she has been wrong several times.

Voice notes for casting: dry, precise and patient. Hesper has a sharp sense of humour and uses long pauses as a weapon.

History: Hesper knew Aldous Ashdown when he was a young man climbing the stair from the Cinder Bridge. She has watched Corin grow into the Bellwarden's role from a distance, and she has written to him only twice in the last ten years.

## Captain Rourke Dale

Captain Rourke Dale has served in the Pass Guard for nineteen winters. He has a grey beard cut short and the shoulders of a man who has carried too many things up too many stairs. His coat is always a little too large for him.

Rourke is tired, honest and drinks more than he should. His want is to keep the town quiet and his men alive through the winter. His flaw is that he drinks to avoid thinking about the things he has seen at the Bell Gate, and it is recorded that his judgement is worst late in the evening.

Voice notes for casting: rough, blunt and slow, with a dry humour that comes out when he is least expecting it. Rourke swears but seldom shouts. When he is lying he becomes more formal, which is the opposite of what most players will expect. Give him long pauses and short answers.

History: Rourke commands the Pass Guard from the barracks beside the Bell Gate. He has known Corin for most of his career and respects him, even when they disagree about the bridge.

## Aldous Ashdown (deceased)

Aldous Ashdown was Corin's father and the Bellwarden before him. He does not appear in the present story, and the archive holds him only in letters, a few tallies and the Order's notes. He is described in those records as broad, quiet and very patient.

Aldous wanted the bell to be kept with honour. His flaw, as the notes suggest, was that he never asked anyone to share the burden of it with him. He died ringing the Ashbell on the Night of Ash, and that single act is the reason the rest of this codex exists. It is recorded that the town still rings the bell in his memory, although nobody now living saw him do it.

Voice notes for casting: none. Aldous is heard only in quotations from his letters, which should be read in a steady, unshowy tone.

History: Aldous taught Corin the bell as he had been taught it himself. Corin keeps his father's tallies in the tower and has never moved them. Hesper remembers Aldous as the only man who ever made her laugh in the cloister, though she will not say what he said.
