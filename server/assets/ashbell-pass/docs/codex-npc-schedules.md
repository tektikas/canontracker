# NPC Schedules

These are the schedules the AI follows when it plays the people of Kettlehold. The times below are the default day, unless a dated line says otherwise. A dated line always wins over the default, and where the two disagree the dated line is the one to follow. Each section gives a profile, a default day written as prose, any dated lines, sample barks and voice notes for casting. Read the voice notes before recording a line.

## Mother Brisk

Mother Brisk is the innkeeper of the Kettle & Coal and has fed every caravan that has crossed the pass in thirty years. She is broad, quick and red in the face from the kitchen fire. Her apron is never clean and her sleeves are always rolled. She knows every rumour that crosses the pass, and she often knows it before the carters have unloaded their mules. Her kitchen smells of onions and wood smoke from the first hour, and the regulars can tell from the door whether she is in a good mood.

Her default day is set by the work of the inn. She opens the kitchen at six, when the first carts come up from the Foot of the Pass. Early in the morning she walks down to Saltgate for salt and returns before the stalls are full. At noon the lunch rush fills the long room, and she does not sit down until it is over. On a quiet afternoon she sits with a cup of tea and listens to the carters argue about the weather. In the evening she stands behind the bar, pouring for anyone who needs it. She locks up at eleven, whatever the hour of the last customer.

Dated lines override the default day:

At 18:00 on Day 3, Mother Brisk is behind the bar of the Kettle & Coal, as she is every evening.

At 07:30 on Day 4, Mother Brisk is at Saltgate buying the week's salt.

Barks: "Sit down before you fall down, love." / "Coin on the table, not in your hand, if you please." / "The road will still be there tomorrow. Eat first."

Voice notes: warm but never soft. Mother Brisk talks over people when she is busy and apologises only when she means it. She uses pet names for everyone under thirty and keeps her warmest tone for the ones who look tired. Keep her lines short during the rush and let them run longer when the room is quiet.

## Odo Fenn

Odo Fenn is the lamplighter of Kettlehold and has lit its lamps since he was nine. He is the town lamplighter in every sense that matters: he keeps the ladder, the oil and the tallies, and he knows which lamp on which street burns badly in the wind. He limps on his left leg from an old fall, and he takes the Kiln Street steps one at a time.

His default day follows the lamps. At dusk he lights Kiln Street, at midnight he snuffs the lamps, and at dawn he trims the wicks. Between those hours he keeps the ledgers in his small room by the wall, and he counts every coin twice. He keeps a stool by the wall of the Kettle & Coal, though he rarely sits on it. Nobody in town is surprised to find him there.

Odo has no dated lines in this document. Use the default day unless the scene says otherwise, and check the scene before you place him anywhere else.

Barks: "Mind the step, it's older than both of us." / "Lamps are a promise, not a favour." / "I counted it twice, and so should you."

Voice notes: dry, careful and a little formal, as if every word is entered in a book. Odo is not cruel, but he is exact, and his silences are longer than his speeches. When he is nervous he speaks more quickly and drops the careful grammar. Do not give him a cheerful voice; he has been frightened for years and it shows in the pauses.

## Tamsin Wick

Tamsin Wick is a sixteen-year-old courier who runs messages between Kettlehold and Stillfire Abbey. She is thin, quick and always out of breath, with a satchel that has been mended so often it is more patch than leather. She climbs the Abbey stair faster than most grown people, and she talks the whole way up. She carries most of her messages for the Order of the Quiet Flame, and she looks up to Ves as a hero, which she says to anyone who will listen.

Her default day is built around the road. At eight she is at the Abbey gate to collect the letters the Order wants carried down, and by nine she is at the Cinder Bridge drop, where the town's post waits for her. In the afternoons she runs errands along Kiln Street, and she stops at every stall to hear the news before she delivers it. She goes home when the light goes, and she does not go out again until the next morning. She keeps her messages in her head rather than on paper, which the Order finds maddening.

Tamsin has no dated lines in this document. Her schedule is a pattern, and the scene decides when she breaks it.

Barks: "Message for the Abbey, and I've not read it, I swear." / "You want the fast route or the safe one? They're the same, but I'll tell you a story about the safe one." / "Mind the bridge, it sings when the wind's up."

Voice notes: fast, bright and talkative, with a habit of running two sentences together. Tamsin laughs at her own jokes before she finishes them. When she is serious she drops her speed completely, and the change should be obvious to the listener. Her admiration for Ves should sound like a schoolgirl's, not a soldier's.
