# S07 — The Ember Well

Sister Agnet unbars the side door of Stillfire Abbey and lets Ves in without a word. The corridor smells of wet stone and of the faint metal breath that rises from the vents below the cloister. Ves still wears the dust of the road, and she is aware of every boot print she leaves on the flagstones. Nobody in the Abbey seems surprised to see a caravan scout at this hour. Agnet lifts her lamp, points down the passage, and turns back to her bolts.

Abbess Hesper Quill is waiting in the small room off the reading hall. She is ninety years old and sits so straight in her stone chair that she seems to have been carved into it. A single candle stands on the table between them, and she does not offer Ves a seat. "You have come to drink from the Well," she says. "Then answer one thing first. Who rang the Ashbell on the Night of Ash?"

Ves thinks of the tower, the smoke over the lower town, and the bell that did not stop until it did. "Aldous Ashdown," she says. "Corin's father. He stayed in the tower."

Hesper tests Ves with a single question about Aldous Ashdown. Ves passes the test without knowing it was one, and the old abbess lets out a slow breath through her nose that might be approval. Ves remembers Aldous Ashdown only as a tall man with a cough and a laugh that carried across the market. She was a girl then, and the lower town was still standing.

"The right answer is a name and nothing more, and you have given it," Hesper says. "Go down. The Well will not wait for your nerves." She turns the candle a little toward the door, which is her way of ending a conversation.

The lower cloister is cut into the rock, and the warm air grows heavier with every step. Ves thinks about the caravan camp at the Foot of the Pass, where she left Old Daisy tethered under a tarp, and about how far the road home has felt since the bell first went quiet. At the far end of the arcade, the Ember Well sits in a basin of black stone. Its surface steams faintly and throws a red light up the pillars. Sister Agnet stops at the edge of that light and does not step any closer.

At 05:00 on Day 3, Ves kneels at the Ember Well and drinks before the sky turns grey. The water is hotter than she expects and tastes of iron and rain. She cups it in both hands and drinks until her throat is raw.

Nothing happens for a long breath. The water burns going down, and when Ves opens her eyes she has Ashsight. The cloister is the same grey stone it was a moment ago, yet a second light lies over it, faint and patient. Every caravan driver who ever drank at this Well told Ves about the burning, and every one of them said it was worth it. None of them mentioned how quiet the room becomes once you can see the heat in it.

Abbess Hesper Quill gives Ves the Cinder Key and tells her to bring it back before the bell rings again. The key is iron, the length of a finger, and it is warm as if someone has been holding it all night. Ves closes her hand around the Cinder Key, warm as a coal, and feels the heat settle into her palm. Hesper watches Ves over the rim of her cup and waits for an answer she has not asked for.

Looking down from the gallery as she leaves, Ves sees the heat of a footprint glow for a heartbeat and then fade. She stops, but the glow does not come back. Her mind keeps the picture anyway: a single pale mark on the stone, and a stride that is not quite even. She decides not to mention it to Agnet, who has already gone back to her bolts.

Outside, the sky over the gorge is still black, and the first cold air of the morning moves up the stair to meet her. Ves tucks the key inside her coat and starts down toward the Cinder Bridge, where the day's first work is waiting. Old Daisy is not with her, and Ves finds she misses the mule's complaints more than she expected to.
