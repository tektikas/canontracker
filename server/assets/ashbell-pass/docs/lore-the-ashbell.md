# The Ashbell

The Ashbell is the great bell of Kettlehold. It is cast of dark bronze, older than the Pass Guard and older than the Order's written records, and it hangs in the top room of the Bell Gate where the whole town can see its shadow at dusk. Its note is low and heavy. It carries down Kiln Street to Saltgate and across the gorge to the Abbey, and on still nights it can be heard far down the high road.

## The duty of the bell

The bell has one duty each day, and it is always the same. At dusk the Bellwarden climbs the tower and rings it. The ringing warns the town of ashfall, when the vents throw grey dust over the roofs, and it marks the hour when the Cinder Bridge is closed for the night. The Pass Guard enforces the curfew on the Cinder Bridge, and no cart crosses the gorge once the bell has sounded.

Every Bellwarden learns the rule of the bell before the first rope is pulled. Whoever rings the *Ashbell* loses their voice until the next sunrise. The Order keeps this as the first lesson of the tower, and it is written in the Bellwarden's own book in the same plain hand used for the lamp-oil ledgers.

## The office of Bellwarden

The Bellwarden is keeper of the Ashbell and warden of the Bell Gate. The office usually passes from parent to child, and it is held with pride and with some dread. A Bellwarden rings at every dusk, in fog and in storm, and reports any fault in the bell to the Pass Guard before the next dusk. The office carries no wage beyond a room in the tower and a share of the lamp oil.

The Bell Gate is a tower of grey stone above the road, and the Pass Guard keeps its barracks beside the gate, so the watch is never far from the bell. The current Bellwarden is Corin Ashdown. He was trained in the tower from childhood, and the Order's records describe him as a careful ringer who keeps the rope well oiled.

## The Night of Ash

On the Night of Ash, ten winters before Ves came home, Aldous Ashdown died ringing the Ashbell. He was the Bellwarden before his son, and he went up the tower when the sky above the summit turned the colour of a bruise. The Order's account says he rang until the rope was torn from his hands.

The Night of Ash buried the lower town of Kettlehold in a single night. The ash came down like wet snow and did not stop. The old streets below Saltgate were filled to the eaves, and the houses on the lower slope were roofed over by morning. People dug for three seasons and found some of what they had lost.

The Order's archivist keeps the records of the Night of Ash, and those records are the only written account the town has of that night. The town does not speak of it at the dusk bell, and the names of the dead are read only once a year.

Children in Kettlehold learn the dusk bell before they learn their letters. They learn to stop their games when the sound rolls down the street, to find a lamp and wait beside it, and to count the strokes under their breath. A child who counts the strokes correctly is said to have a good ear for ash, and a child who does not is told to listen harder. Most families keep a small brass bell of their own on the doorstep, rung only at a funeral or a wedding, so that the town's great bell is never mistaken for a household sound.

## What the Order teaches

The Order teaches that the bell is a debt rather than a tool. It does not ring the bell and does not explain the rule to visitors. It says only that the bell answers to those who pay for it, and it asks each Bellwarden to weigh the price before taking the rope.

Visitors who study the bell often expect a grand story. There is no grand story. There is a rope, a tower, a town that listens at dusk, and a debt that every ringer must carry for as long as the rope is in their hands.
