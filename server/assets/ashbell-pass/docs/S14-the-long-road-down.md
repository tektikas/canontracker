# S14 — The Long Road Down

Day 5 starts in silence, and then, at sunrise, it gives her a word back. Ves says "Corin" out loud in the room above the Kettle & Coal. It comes out rough and cracked, like a hinge nobody has oiled in years, but it is hers. She says it again to be sure. Then she laughs, and the laugh hurts her ribs, and she does not care.

The room is cold, and the window shows the first grey light over the gorge. Mother Brisk has left bread and a cup of tea on the table, and a note in Pell's large letters that says EAT. Ves eats standing up, looking out at the steam. She thinks about what she will say first when someone asks her to speak in public. She decides she will say nothing unless it is worth the breath.

On Day 5, Ves climbs to Stillfire Abbey to return the Cinder Key. The path is steep and wet with spray from the gorge, and the rock is warm through the soles of her boots. She climbs slowly, one hand on the rail, and stops twice to catch her breath. Nobody hurries her. The key sits in her coat pocket, and every few steps it knocks against her hip as if it wants to be noticed.

When she was small, Corin took her up a path like this and taught her to read the warm marks the steam left on the stones. He would stop and point, and she would be too proud to ask what he meant. She remembers that now, and she does not stop to think about why.

At the Abbey gate, Sister Agnet looks at her for a long moment through the hatch before she lets her in. She does not ask about the voice, and Ves is grateful for that. Inside, the cloisters are quiet except for the drip of water somewhere out of sight. The air is thick and warm, and it smells faintly of metal.

Hesper waits for her in the lower cloister, beside the Ember Well, where the water steams in a stone basin. The old woman has a blanket over her knees and a cup of something hot in both hands. She looks Ves up and down, then at the key in Ves's open palm.

"Come here, child, and do not make me stand up." Hesper holds out her hand. Ves places the key in it, and the old woman closes her fingers over it without ceremony. "You will not need me again, and that is the best thing I can say to anyone who has walked this road."

Ves nods. She cannot answer, so she touches her throat and then points at the Ember Well, and something almost like a smile moves across Hesper's mouth. "Go down. Eat something. The road will be long enough without you fainting on it." The old woman turns her face back to the steam, and that is the end of the audience.

Ves stays a moment longer by the basin. The water is hot enough to hurt if she dips her fingers, and she does not try. She wonders, not for the first time, whether the Order keeps the Well for itself or only for people who ask in the right way. She has never asked in any way at all. Perhaps that is why the Well gave her what it did, and perhaps it was only the cold that made her eyes water. She leaves the cloister without deciding which.

The walk down is quicker than the climb and no less tiring. Ves comes out through the gate with the steam at her back and the town spread out below her, roofs and smoke and the thin line of Kiln Street running down toward Saltgate. She stops by the rail to look at it properly, for the first time since she came home. It looks smaller than it did six years ago, and also more crowded.

At 10:00 on Day 5, Ves stands at the Bell Gate and watches the Red Mule Caravan go.

Corin stands beside her with his arm in a sling and says nothing, which is his way of saying a great deal. The sling is tight across his chest, and he keeps his good hand on the rail. He does not look at her once. He does not need to. They have stood at this gate together before, and neither of them has ever been the one who left.

The town has come out to watch, though nobody cheers. A few children sit on the low wall by the gate with their hands shading their eyes against the morning glare. Two guards stand under the arch and pretend not to look at Ves, and a salt trader near the back of the crowd keeps his cap in his hands the whole time, as if he were at a funeral. Nobody says a word to her. She is grateful for the quiet, because she has no word to give back.

The wagons roll out under the gate one by one, their wheels loud on the stones, the mules tossing their heads at the steam. Ysolde Marr rides at the front and does not look back, and Ves cannot tell whether that is pride or shame. Her hand goes to her throat, out of habit, the way it did when she was small and wanted to call across a crowded square. It stays there until the last wagon is through.

Old Daisy noses at her pocket for a sugar lump that is not there. Ves rubs the mule's neck and says nothing at all. The caravan dust hangs over the road for a while and then thins and drifts apart, and the sound of the wheels fades into the ordinary noise of the town.

Ves thinks about what she will say to Ysolde when there is a voice to say it with. Anger, probably. Something sharper than that, and sadder. She decides it will keep until the road is quiet, and until she has found out what the broken oath actually cost, and who else paid for it. The thought does not settle her, but it gives her hands something to do.

At dusk the town goes quiet, the way it always does before the warning. The clapper is back in place, and the tower looks the way it always has. Ves stands on Kiln Street while the lamps are lit one by one, and she waits. The Ashbell speaks once, low and clean, and somewhere below in the dusk a voice goes quiet for the night.

## Ending: Stay

Ves turns from the gate and walks back down Kiln Street with Old Daisy at her shoulder. She takes the room above the Kettle & Coal for now and asks Mother Brisk for work that needs no bell at all. Mother Brisk, who knows every rumour, says she has already heard. The town needs someone who can read the heat-trails, and Ves finds that she does not mind the idea. She ties the mule to the rail outside and goes in to eat.

## Ending: Follow

Ves unties Old Daisy from the rail and climbs onto her back. She catches the last wagon at the gate and rides up the line to the front, where Ysolde Marr is waiting with an expression Ves cannot read. Whatever is said between them is said quietly. The road ahead is long enough for both of them, and neither one is in any hurry to finish the conversation.
