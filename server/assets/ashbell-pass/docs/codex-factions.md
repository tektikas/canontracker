# Factions of the Pass

It is recorded that three bodies shape life on the Ashbell Pass: a monastic order, a town watch and a trading caravan. Each has its own goals, its own leader and its own view of the others. The Order keeps the old heat in check, the Guard keeps the peace, and the caravan keeps the town fed. The Archive notes that trouble usually starts where their interests meet.

## The Order of the Quiet Flame

Abbess Hesper Quill leads the Order of the Quiet Flame from Stillfire Abbey. She is the oldest voice in the Pass, and visitors often leave her presence feeling that she has already read their letters.

The Order's goal is to tend the Ember Well and to seal places where old heat is dangerous. Its members keep a ledger of every sealed door, and the ledger is never shown to outsiders. The Order believes that a sealed door should stay sealed, however politely the town asks.

The Order regards the Pass Guard as a useful tool and the Caravan as a necessary evil. It does not forbid trade, but it will not open a sealed place for money. Its stance toward the Bell Gate is one of quiet distance: it sends word when it must, and it rarely sends more.

Rumour holds that the Order keeps a second set of keys in a cellar beneath the Abbey. The Archive has no record of any such cellar, and the archivist finds that silence more troubling than the rumour itself.

Visitors who ask the Order for help with a sealed place are sent first to the lower cloister, where a sister reads their request aloud. Only when the request is repeated word for word is it taken to the Abbess. The Order says this keeps fools out, and the Guard says it keeps the Order busy.

## The Pass Guard

Captain Rourke Dale commands the Pass Guard from the barracks beside the Bell Gate. He is tired, honest and, by the account of his own sergeants, fond of a drink after the evening watch. The Guard keeps the town watch, enforces the bridge curfew and posts a patrol along Kiln Street.

The Pass Guard answers to the Abbess in matters of fire and to no one in matters of snow. The Guard's own rule book says so in its first line, and few captains have ever argued with it.

The Guard respects the Order's seals and resents the caravan's special passes. Its men say the Caravan buys its way through the gate, and the caravan's men say the Guard only counts what it can see.

The barracks say the Captain has not been fully sober at the change of watch for a long while. Nobody has written it down, and nobody has been brave enough to ask him.

The Guard's duties are plain. Its men walk the Bell Gate at dusk, count the lamps on Kiln Street, and report every open door to the captain before the watch changes. Recruits learn the bridge curfew before they learn the names of the men beside them, and the older guards say the Pass has never once been quiet at night.

## The Red Mule Caravan

Captain Ysolde Marr leads the Red Mule Caravan. She is charming, well connected and in debt to more than one lender. The Caravan pays the Pass Guard for safe passage through the Bell Gate, and it keeps a standing purse for bad weather.

The Red Mule Caravan carries salt and lamp oil up the pass twice a year. The manifests list nothing else, and the clerks at Saltgate check every crate against them.

Its goal is steady profit and a road that stays open. Its drivers are loyal to the mules first and to the captain second, a joke that the caravan's own people tell with pride. The caravan trades with both the Order and the Guard, and it keeps its quarrels for the inn.

Ysolde's ember-salt smuggling is only a rumour here. Traders in the lower town talk of a second load that never reaches the scales, and nobody will say who pays for it. The Archive records the rumour and nothing more.

The caravan's routes are kept in a leather book that the captain never lets out of her sight. Drivers say the book has three pages more than any honest manifest needs, and they laugh when asked which pages those are.
