# S11 — The Dawn Bell

The clapper goes back onto its hook long after the town has gone quiet. Ves and Corin lift the Bronze Clapper back onto its hook in the dark, and neither of them says much. The iron is colder and heavier than she remembers. Corin checks every bolt with two fingers, slowly, the way a man checks a sum he has already done once.

The tower stair is narrow and wet with the steam that seeps up through the cracks. Corin goes first, one hand on the rope rail. Halfway down he misses a step. Corin falls on the stone stair of the tower and breaks his arm. He does not cry out. He hits the wall, then sits, and when Ves reaches him his left arm is at an angle no arm should take.

Ves wraps the arm in her own scarf and splints it with two slats from a broken crate that someone left on the landing. Corin props his broken arm against the wall and says nothing. His jaw works, and sweat runs into his beard. He does not ask her to fetch anyone, and she does not offer. There is no one on the stair who could carry him down faster than he can walk.

The slats are damp and splintered at one end, and she has to smooth the rough edge with her thumbnail before she dares to tie them on. Corin winces once, then goes still. She knows he is counting his breaths the way he taught her to on the caravan road, in, hold, out, as if the rhythm alone could keep the pain in its place.

Outside the narrow window the first grey light has begun to show over the gorge, though there is no sun on the stones yet. Ves knows what the morning is for. She stands at the foot of the rope, and her palms feel the rough hemp before she has touched it.

At 06:00 on Day 4, Ves rings the Ashbell herself, and the sound rolls all the way down to the Foot of the Pass. Ves pulls the rope with both hands and leans her weight into it. The bell swings, the clapper strikes, and the note goes out over the roofs in a wave that she can feel in her teeth.

Her voice goes out in the middle of a breath, and she tastes iron. She tries to call out to Corin and nothing comes. Her throat closes like a fist. Corin holds Ves by the shoulder with his good hand and does not let go, and she is grateful for it, even though she can no longer tell him so.

For a long moment after the note fades, nobody moves. The stones of the tower hum faintly under her boots, and she can feel the echo in the floor long after she can hear it. Ves thinks about the caravan road, about the nights she spent with her back against a wagon wheel, listening to the drivers argue about the price of salt. None of them ever mentioned what the bell would take from the one who rang it.

Ves has no words for what she has done. She has rung the bell so the whole pass can hear it, and the price was never written anywhere she could read it.

Corin's face shows that he knows the price of the rope. He does not look surprised. He looks like a man who has been waiting for a debt to come due, and who has just watched it arrive. He says nothing, because there is nothing he can say that would change what she has paid. Ves sees the same understanding in his eyes that she feels in her own chest, and she wishes, for once, that she did not recognise it.

Below the tower, the town is starting to stir. Shutters open one by one along Kiln Street, and a dog barks at the empty square. Ves leans against the wall beside Corin and listens to the echo fade, and she does not try to speak again.

Dust hangs in the grey light from the window, and somewhere beneath them a cart creaks past in the lower street. Ves draws her knees up and rests her head against the cold stone. Corin's good hand stays on her shoulder, heavy and steady, and neither of them moves to leave the tower yet.
