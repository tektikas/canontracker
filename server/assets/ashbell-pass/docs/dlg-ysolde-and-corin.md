# Dialogue: Ysolde and Corin

Day 2, evening. The foot of the Bell Gate, in the cold light of the lamps on the wall. CORIN and YSOLDE speak. The Pass Guard stands at the edge of the light and does not interfere. Ves is out of sight behind the stable wall and hears most of it.

(Ysolde comes up the slope from the caravan lines with her coat open. Corin is already waiting at the gate, with his back to the tower.)

YSOLDE: You kept me standing in the wind, Corin. I thought you would at least give me the courtesy of a chair.

CORIN: I gave you the courtesy of my time. That is more than most people in this town get from me now.

YSOLDE: Then let me use it well. I have a caravan of ember salt waiting at the Foot of the Pass, and the wagons cannot climb the stair before the lamps go out.

CORIN: Then the wagons will wait, as they always have. Salt does not spoil in one night.

YSOLDE: Just once, Corin. Leave the bridge open for one night and I will never ask again. You have my word on that, and you know what my word is worth when it is good.

(Corin does not answer straight away. He looks up at the bell tower, where the Ashbell hangs black against the sky.)

CORIN: Your word was worth a great deal, once. It was worth more than mine, if you want the truth of it. We made our oath on the same bell, you and I, and we stood under it like fools who thought it would last.

YSOLDE: It did last. It lasted until you decided that a town matters more than the people who keep it running.

CORIN: My father rang that bell with his last breath, and he never once asked the bridge to wait for anyone. I will not be the Ashdown who breaks that.

(A gust of wind pushes the gate chain against the iron, a dull sound like a bell with no voice. Both of them flinch, then pretend they did not.)

YSOLDE: You still do that, Corin. You flinch at every noise this gate makes, as if you were waiting for someone to come up the stair and tell you it was over.

CORIN: Someone always comes up the stair. That is the job. The bell is the only thing in Kettlehold that does not lie about it.

YSOLDE: The bell lies about one thing, and you know which. It takes a voice from the one who rings it, and it does not ask first.

CORIN: It never asks. That is why a Bellwarden has to be willing before the first note.

(He lays one hand on the cold iron of the gate. Ysolde counts the lamps on the wall one by one, as if she is working out a debt.)

YSOLDE: Willing. You were always willing to be a fool for a dead man's rope.

CORIN: My father would have let the wagons through and then sat in the tower all night with a cup of cold tea, waiting for the Pass Guard to come and ask him why. He never did anything by halves, and neither will I.

(A lamp hisses somewhere on the wall. The Pass Guard shifts his feet and looks at the gravel.)

YSOLDE: Your father is dead, Corin. He is dead, and the bridge is still a bridge. Stop hiding behind his name every time you have a choice to make.

CORIN: The bridge closes when the bell rings. It always has.

YSOLDE: Then I’m done with oaths — yours and mine.

(She turns to go, then turns back. Her voice drops, and for a moment it sounds like the woman who used to laugh at this gate with him.)

YSOLDE: Odo owes me, and he will pay me one way or another. Tell your limping friend with the tallies that the debt is older than the bell you keep.

CORIN: I will not open the bridge for salt or for love. You can tell that to the wagons, and to the men who drive them.

(Ysolde walks back down the slope without looking at the gate. Corin stays where he is until the lamps on the wall go out one by one.)

(The Pass Guard clears his throat and asks whether he should walk the captain home. Corin says no. He will go up the tower on his own.)

CORIN: Let the caravan take the long road, Sergeant. If anyone asks, the bridge is shut for the night, and that is the whole of my answer.

(Ysolde is already on the caravan path. She does not look back, and the cold air carries her last words up to the wall.)

YSOLDE: Then you will see what a broken oath costs this town, and you will be the one who pays it.
