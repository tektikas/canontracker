# S02 — Dusk at the Bell Gate

The tower stair is steep and narrow, and it smells of tallow and cold stone. Ves follows Corin up it with her pack left at the bottom, because he told her to come up light. He climbs without touching the rail. She had forgotten how he does that, and she is irritated to find that her own hand has gone to the rail anyway.

The light outside is turning amber. From the top window the whole of Kettlehold lies below them: the roofs, the steam, the long line of Kiln Street with its lamps being lit one by one, and beyond it the gorge, where the mist lies thick and white.

"Hold the rope with both hands," Corin says. "Not one. Both. It pulls harder than you think."

"I remember."

"You remember what you were told at twelve."

The rope comes down through a square hole in the ceiling and winds around a wooden wheel. The groove in the wheel is worn smooth, and it is older than Corin. Ves wraps her palms around the rope the way she was taught and waits for the signal.

Below them the town goes quiet in the way she remembers. Doors close. A dog stops barking halfway through a bark. The caravan hands at the bottom of Kiln Street pull their hats down and stop talking, and nobody tells them to.

Down in the lower town a door bangs open and someone calls a name. Then the caller thinks better of it and the door shuts again. Ves looks down, but there is nothing to see except the steam, pale against the roofs, and the first lamp of the evening coming alight at the top of Kiln Street.

At 19:00 on Day 1, Corin rings the Ashbell and the whole valley holds its breath.

The sound comes up through the floor first, through Ves's boots and into her teeth, and then it fills the tower until there is no room left in it for anything else. She has heard the bell every evening of her childhood. She has never once stood this close to it, and she has never once felt it in her ribs.

As a girl she used to count the seconds between the swing and the note, and she lost count every time, because the note came up through the stone and she forgot what she was counting. Corin made her sit on the bottom step while he rang, so that she would learn to feel the bell instead of hearing it. She sat on that step for a whole winter before she understood what he meant. Her palms are damp on the rope now, and she wishes she had listened sooner.

Corin does not look at her while the bell rings. His eyes are on the gorge, and his jaw works once, then again, as if he is counting something under his breath.

Corin shouts a warning down to the Pass Guard the moment the last note fades.

He leans out over the rail and bellows toward the barracks, and his voice cracks off the stone. He tells the Pass Guard that there is ash on the wind and the lanterns are to stay shuttered. Sergeant Fennick answers from below. Ves hears the sergeant's voice and then Corin's again, and she thinks only that he has always had a carrying voice.

"Your father used to stand exactly here," Ves says when he comes back in.

Corin's hand tightens on the rail. "He stood here until the night he did not come down," he says. "Don't."

She does not, though she wants to. Aldous Ashdown is a name the town still says carefully, the way people step around a bad board on a stair. Ves has heard it said in every tone there is, and she has never once heard Corin say it himself.

The tower is cold, and the cold gets into the joints of her fingers. Corin pulls his coat tighter without comment. Through the window the caravan's cook fires are being lit on the slope below the tower, small and orange, and a dog somewhere in the lower town starts barking again, as if it has been waiting for permission. Ves finds she is counting the fires, the way she counted the lamps, and she loses her place twice.

Far below, under the mist, iron grinds on iron. The gates of the Cinder Bridge are closing for the night, one leaf and then the other, and the sound climbs the gorge wall the way the bell did. Corin does not turn to watch. He is watching the road, and the last of the light, and the dark shape of the Kiln Street lamps going out one after another.
