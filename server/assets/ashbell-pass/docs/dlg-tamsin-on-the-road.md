# Dialogue: Tamsin on the Road

Optional companion banter. Day 3, on the path up from Kiln Street toward the Cinder Bridge, after a short walk from the Bell Gate. Ves walks, and Tamsin walks faster, talking the whole way. Speakers: TAMSIN, with VES. Play it only if Tamsin is in the party.

(Tamsin skips ahead on the loose stones and looks back over her shoulder with her braid flying.)

TAMSIN: You are slow for a scout, Ves. I thought the caravan road made people quick. Or did six years of rest make you lazy?

VES: The caravan road makes people tired. Quick comes later, when someone is chasing you.

TAMSIN: Nobody chases a courier. We are too boring to be worth the trouble.

(Ves gives a short laugh. Old Daisy, the mule, snorts behind her and refuses to hurry.)

TAMSIN: Look at her. She knows the way to the bridge better than you do. I should take her job, and you could carry the letters for once.

VES: Old Daisy has walked this road more times than either of us has. She stays where she likes.

TAMSIN: I carry letters up to the Abbey for half the town, and I have never once been late. Hesper says so herself, and she does not praise anyone who has not earned it.

VES: Hesper praised you?

TAMSIN: She gave me a sweet from the jar on her desk and said I had good legs for stairs. That is practically a medal from the Order. I have been telling everyone.

(They pass a stack of ash-grey sacks by the wall. Tamsin tugs one aside, peers at the stamp, and lets it fall back.)

TAMSIN: Everyone at the Abbey gate says the Ember Well is haunted now. The novices whisper about it every night. I do not believe a word, but I listen anyway.

VES: What do they whisper?

TAMSIN: That the water is hot enough to scald a bird out of the air, and that the sisters keep the lower cloister locked for a reason. They never say the reason. That is the best part of any rumour.

(Ves stops at the rail above the gorge. The steam rises between the rocks like breath on a cold window.)

TAMSIN: You have been gone six years, Ves. I was a kid with scraped knees when you left, and now I am the one who carries your news up the hill.

VES: You were always the one who carried news.

TAMSIN: That is because nobody else in this town will run. Old Odo walks, the Pass Guard marches, and the crier shouts. Only the couriers run.

(Tamsin leans on the rail beside her and stares down into the steam.)

TAMSIN: I would sign on with the Red Mule tomorrow if Ysolde would have me. Salt in one hand, a whip in the other, and the road in front of me every morning. That is what I want.

VES: The Red Mule is not a children's game, Tamsin. The road has teeth.

TAMSIN: So do I. Ask anyone who has tried to steal a letter from my satchel.

(She lifts the satchel strap and grins. Ves laughs properly this time, and the sound surprises both of them.)

“Out here you’re my sister of the road,” Tamsin tells Ves, though they share no blood at all.

VES: You are not my sister, Tam. You are a courier with a big mouth.

TAMSIN: Then I will be your courier with a big mouth, and you can keep the mule. I trust you with my letters, and that is more than I trust most of the town.

(A cart of lamp oil rattles past on the lower road. The driver tips his hat to Tamsin as if she were a grown woman with a wagon of her own, and she takes the salute as her due.)

(The path turns toward the bridge. Tamsin starts walking again, quicker now, and calls back over the rail.)

TAMSIN: Come on, scout. The bridge will not wait for us, and I crossed it this morning before the sun was up. You have some catching up to do.
