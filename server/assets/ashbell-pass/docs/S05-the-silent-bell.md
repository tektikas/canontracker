# S05 — The Silent Bell

The whole of Kettlehold gathers at the foot of the Bell Gate as the light fails. Nobody has been told to come, and everybody has come anyway. Ves stands among them with her hands in her pockets and her breath steaming in the cold. The lamps on Kiln Street are being lit one by one below her, and the gorge sends up a smell of hot iron. Nobody speaks. Children have been pulled back against their mothers' skirts, and the guards at the gate keep their eyes on the tower.

Corin climbs the tower stair two steps at a time. Ves watches the shape of him pass the narrow windows, one after another, until he reaches the bell chamber. A moment later the rope above the door goes taut, and the great bronze mass begins to move. The Ashbell rocks in its frame. It swings out over the town, hangs at the top of its arc, and swings back.

At dusk on Day 2, the Ashbell swings but makes no sound at all.

For a heartbeat nobody understands what they have heard, or failed to hear. Then a woman near the front cries out, and the cry runs through the crowd like a crack through ice. Men shout that the bridge must be closed. Someone runs toward the gorge, and someone else runs after him. Nobody closes the Cinder Bridge. Its gate is still standing open, and the bell that should have dropped it for the night hangs silent above their heads.

Ves stands still and makes herself breathe. She has seen panic in the caravan camps, and she knows that the first person who moves with purpose can stop it. She does not move. She waits until Corin's face appears in the tower window, pale and fixed, and then she climbs the stair after him.

The bell chamber smells of oil and cold stone. The Ashbell hangs in its frame, and its great lip is still. Corin stands beneath it, staring up at the iron crown where the clapper should hang. The chain is there, but it is empty. Its last link is polished bright where the weight used to rest, and nothing hangs from it at all. The Bronze Clapper is gone.

Ves looks around the chamber for anything out of place. The floor has been swept recently, and the oil lamp on its hook is still warm. A scrap of rope lies against the wall, cut clean, and she turns it over in her fingers without knowing what it means yet. Below them the crowd is still shouting. The sound drifts up the stairwell muffled and far away, like a conversation on the other side of a wall.

"Who was up here?" Ves asks.

Corin does not answer straight away. "No one," he says at last. "The door was barred from the inside all afternoon. I barred it myself."

Ves follows him back down the stairs. Captain Rourke is waiting at the bottom with two of his guards and a face like wet clay. He has been on watch since the lamps were lit, and he is sure of what he saw. "I was watching the tower all night," Rourke says. "Nobody climbed it." He says it twice, as if the second time might make it true for the people behind him.

His guards shift their feet and look at the paving rather than at Ves. One of them, a young man with a burn across his knuckles, keeps glancing up at the tower as if he expects the bell to ring of its own accord. Nobody corrects him. Nobody wants to be the first to say what they are all thinking, which is that the Ashbell has never failed before, not in anyone's memory.

From 21:00 to 22:00 on Day 2, Captain Rourke stands watch at the Bell Gate and sees no one climb the tower.

Ves believes him, or wants to. She does not say so. Instead she looks back up at the tower, at the narrow windows, the dark stair, and the one door that is barred from within. Someone got in, and someone got out again. Nobody has ever failed to climb that tower without being seen.

Corin finds her later, when the crowd has thinned and the lamps are steady. He stands close, and his voice is quiet. "Find out who took it," he says. "I need someone who can walk where the guards cannot."

"Why me?"

"Because you are the only one who will not ask me for permission."

Ves looks at him for a long moment. She does not like the request, and she does not like how much he needs it. Still, she nods. Somewhere below them a woman is weeping, and a dog is barking at nothing down the gorge. The bell above them keeps its silence, and Ysolde is nowhere in the crowd. Ves notices that, and she files it away with everything else.
