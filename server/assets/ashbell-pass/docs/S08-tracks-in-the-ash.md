# S08 — Tracks in the Ash

The morning comes up grey over the gorge, and the steam vents breathe long white plumes that drift across the stair. At 09:00 on Day 3, Tamsin is waiting at the Cinder Bridge with a message from the Abbey. Ves sees her before she sees Ves: a thin figure in a patched coat, bouncing on the rail as if the iron were a springboard.

Tamsin is sixteen, quick, and never still for long. She spots Ves coming off the stair, skids across the wet planks, and presses a folded message into Ves's hand. Then she talks without pausing for breath. "Abbey's orders," she says. "It's short, so don't make a face. I've got two more runs before the vents shift, so I can't wait for an answer."

The message is written in a careful hand, and it says only three words: keep your eyes open. Ves reads it twice. The paper is still warm from Tamsin's coat. Then she runs back up the stair without waiting for an answer, her boots ringing on the steps until the sound is swallowed by the steam.

Ves folds the note into her pocket, beside the Cinder Key, which still hums faintly against her ribs. She has been carrying the key for less than an hour, and already it feels heavier than its size. She waits for her breathing to settle before she does anything with the sight that is now hers.

Ves tests her new sight on Kiln Street, where the lamps are cold and unlit at this hour. The street runs steeply downhill from the gate, and its cobbles are slick from the night's fog. She stands at the top of the slope and lets her eyes go soft, the way the caravan drivers described it, and the world changes. Ves sees fading heat-prints on the cobbles of Kiln Street, each one a pale ghost of someone who walked there after the last bell.

Ves looks up at the Bell Gate tower, where the great bell hangs silent above the town. Its shadow lies across the top of the street, and no warmth comes from the tower at all, which is strange. Every other stone in Kettlehold seems to hold some heat, but the bell tower is cold as a grave.

The heat trail leads from the Bell Gate tower down Kiln Street toward the Old Mine. The prints are faint but steady, and each one has a warm heel and a lighter toe. It is a limping stride, one foot pressed harder than the other, and it keeps to the middle of the cobbles where a man would walk if he did not want to be seen from the windows. Ves follows the limp down the hill, one print at a time, and does not hurry.

A Pass Guard patrol passes the bottom of Kiln Street and does not look up. Ves keeps to the wall and waits until their lanterns turn the corner. The guards are talking about the bridge curfew and about someone who was supposed to be on watch the night before, but she does not catch the name. When the lanterns are gone, the heat trail is still there, untouched by the boots.

The trail bends toward the Tallow Stair, a narrow flight off Kiln Street that Ves has not climbed since she was a girl. Its steps are worn into shallow dishes by generations of feet, and the prints fill every dish. Ves stops on the third step and looks back up at the tower, which is still silent, and then down at the street, which is full of ordinary morning noise: shutters opening, a cart wheel complaining, a door slamming two houses away.

She sets her foot in the next print and feels it give a little, the way old wood does. The sight shows her one last warm mark on the stone, then the cold begins to take it back. Ves keeps going down, because the trail is still there and the day is young, and because the message in her pocket says to keep her eyes open.
