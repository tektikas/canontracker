# Roads, Bridges and Travel Times

It is recorded that every road into Kettlehold climbs the same steep line of switchbacks. The Archive has walked each one, and its notes describe the roads as the archivists found them, from the foot of the mountain to the top of the town.

## The Road Up

On Day 0, the Red Mule Caravan makes camp at the Foot of the Pass. The drivers unhitch the mules, check the ropes and sleep in shifts, because the road above the camp is steep and the nights are cold.

The Foot of the Pass lies half a day below Kettlehold, and the last of the grass ends there. Above the camp the road narrows, and the snow lies longer on the north side of every bend.

## Kiln Street

Kiln Street runs down from the Bell Gate toward Saltgate, lined with lamps. The lamps are lit at dusk and put out late, and the lamplighter's tallies are kept in the Archive. Traffic is heavy on the lower end, where the stalls spill into the road.

Snow slows every caravan on Kiln Street, and the lamplighters work longer hours. The Guard asks drivers to walk their mules on the steep cobbles and to keep the wheels off the ice.

## The Cinder Bridge

The Cinder Bridge spans the steam gorge between Kiln Street and the Abbey stair. Its iron deck is warm to the touch in winter, and the steam rises through its grating in long white plumes.

The Cinder Bridge closes at dusk, when the bell rings, and it stays shut until dawn. A traveller caught on the far side must wait at the Abbey stair or find a bed in the lower cloister. The Pass Guard keeps the bar and the key to the lower gate.

## The Stair to the Abbey

Stillfire Abbey sits above the steam gorge, reached by a switchback stair from the far end of the bridge. The climb from the Cinder Bridge to Stillfire Abbey takes twenty minutes on the switchback stair. Walkers who go up in the dark carry a lantern, and the stair's third landing has a bench for the weary.

Sister Agnet keeps the door at the top and asks every visitor for a name, a reason and a promise to walk slowly. Most visitors give all three without argument.

## Snow and Delays

Snow delays every road on the mountain. The Guard closes the Bell Gate road when the drifts are above a man's knee. The caravan waits at the Foot of the Pass until the drifts settle. A caravan that tries the road in deep snow loses its mules before it loses its time.

The walk from the Bell Gate to Saltgate takes forty-five minutes — longer in snow. Plan for the longer walk whenever the sky over the summit turns grey.

## For Level Design

Use the bridge as the hinge of the map. Kiln Street is the spine, the Abbey stair is the climb above the gorge, and the Old Mine lies below the bridge where a player can see the deck from the rail. Keep every route readable at a glance, and let the snow change the pace of a journey rather than its distance.
