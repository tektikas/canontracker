# S10 — Saltgate at Dusk

Saltgate is loud in the afternoon, and Ves has to push to get through it. Carts loaded with lamp oil roll over the cobbles, and a boy shouts prices for dried apples from a stall with no roof. The square smells of wet stone and of the sulphur that drifts up from the vents under the old wall. Ves keeps her hood down so people can see the scout's brand on her coat, and she keeps her eyes on the crowd instead of the buildings.

At 14:10 on Day 3, Ves is pushing through the crowd at Saltgate, looking for Ysolde. She has been walking since the morning with a pack that is too heavy and a mule that refuses to hurry. Old Daisy stands tied at a post near the fountain, chewing and unimpressed. Ves does not stop to greet anyone she knows. Every face she passes is either one from six years ago or one she half remembers from a story told at a campfire, and most of them look away first.

Ember salt changes hands here under the counter. A trader in a grey apron lifts a sack from the back of a cart and shows the crystals to a man in a fur collar, then lowers the flap before anyone else can see. Ves knows the trick. Her caravan used it on the road for years. She can tell by the way the man keeps one hand on his purse that the price is already agreed, and only the weight is left to argue about.

Ves stops at the rope where the mule-carts are queued. Ysolde's mules are there, six of them, their packs already strapped. Two of the drivers are laughing about a tavern in the lower town, and one of them looks up at Ves, then back at the load. Nobody says Ysolde's name aloud. The Red Mule brand is burned into every pack saddle, and Ves knows it the way she knows her own handwriting.

Ysolde is not with them. Ves waits by the rope for a long time, long enough for the light on the square to turn from white to yellow. The drivers keep looking toward the far end, where the old wall meets the alley that runs down toward the water. Ves decides they are looking at something, and she follows the look.

Around evening on Day 3, Mother Brisk is at Saltgate haggling over a barrel of ember salt. She stands at the edge of the market stalls behind her inn, a short, wide woman with flour on her sleeves, and she speaks to the barrel's owner as if they are old enemies. The owner shrugs. Mother Brisk taps the lid twice, pays half of what he asks, and waves a boy over to roll the barrel toward the back door of the Kettle & Coal. Ves watches from the far side of the stalls and does not go over. Brisk would see her at once, and Ves is not ready to be recognised yet.

Ves turns back to the mule-carts. The loading has changed. Ysolde's mules are moving now, the packs roped down, the drivers pulling the lead animals into a line toward the lower gate. No one blows a horn. The carts go quietly, the way a caravan leaves a town that would rather not notice. Ves moves to cut across, and by the time she reaches the gate the last mule is turning the corner.

Ysolde is there for a moment. She stands at the edge of the lamp light with her collar up and her hat low, and she looks straight at Ves. Ves lifts a hand. Ysolde does not lift hers. She steps back into the dark of the alley, and the crowd closes over the place where she stood. When Ves pushes through, there is only a wet print of hooves on the stones and the smell of lamp oil.

The night run is going to happen anyway. Ves can see it in the way the drivers check their knots and then the sky. Ysolde does not need the square to run a load; she needs it to be empty of questions. Ves has known that for years, and she has never been able to decide whether it is a comfort or a warning.

Far above the rooftops, at the top of town, the bell tower is dark. No lamp burns in its windows. Ves stops in the middle of the square to look up at it. The quiet after the noise of the market is strange, and for a moment she has the feeling that the whole square is waiting, the way a crowd waits for a speaker to start. Then the carts are gone, the stalls are being shuttered, and Ves is alone with her pack and her questions.
