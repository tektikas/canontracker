# S09 — The Old Mine

By late morning on Day 3, the heat trail has brought Ves to the foot of the old workings. The gorge wall here is scarred with rusted rails, and the mouth of the mine is a black timber frame sunk deep into the rock. Steam from a hidden vent curls out of the gap and tastes of sulphur on the back of the tongue.

The door is iron-bound and wider than a cart. A chain runs through its hasp, and the chain has been painted so many times that the paint has cracked into a grey net. Someone once tried to burn the lock, and the scorch marks still show on the frame. Ves tests the chain. It does not move, and neither does the lock beneath it.

Ves turns the Cinder Key and the sealed door of the Old Mine sighs open. The lock is old, but the key fits it as if it were made for this one door alone. The warmth in the iron seems to run into the hinges, and the door swings out with a long, tired groan that echoes off the gorge walls and dies away below.

Beyond the door, the dark is total for three steps. Then it softens, and the shapes of the tunnel come up out of the black. Ves uses Ashsight to find the way through the Old Mine, where the heat of old footsteps is thick on the floor. Some of those prints are days old and some are years old, and she does not try to tell them apart. She walks into the dark with the key still warm in her fist.

Old ore carts sit on the rails, rusted in place and tipped against each other like tired animals. Ember salt crusts the walls in pale yellow veins and smells of struck flint. Two empty sacks lie folded by the wall, and a lamp hook hangs from a beam with no lamp on it. Ves wonders how long it has been since anyone worked this seam, and whether anyone has worked it quietly since.

At the junction of the main gallery, a wooden board hangs on two nails. It is covered in names and times, written in chalk and wiped and written again. Ves reads the sign-in board at the junction, slowly, because the chalk is faint and some of the names have been smeared by a sleeve. The chalk is old, but the letters are clear, and she reads them twice before she believes them.

The sign-in board shows that Odo Fenn was at the Old Mine at 22:00 on Day 2. The name is written in a neat, square hand, and the time sits beside it as if the writer wanted no argument. Ves thinks of the man who lights the lamps on Kiln Street, and of the limp she followed down the hill. She does not say the name aloud, because the tunnel carries sound too well, and because she is not yet sure who else might be listening.

Ves checks the rails and the gaps between the carts with the care of someone who has been told not to touch anything. Wrapped in sacking behind the ore carts lies the Bronze Clapper. The bundle is heavy and cold at one end, and the sacking has gone stiff with old grease. She kneels, lifts one corner, and sees the curved bronze under the cloth. Then she covers it again without a sound.

The clapper is the length of her forearm and heavier than it looks. She hauls the bundle onto her shoulder and starts back toward the light. The climb out takes all of her strength and then some, and every step up the gorge path makes the sacking slip a little on her back. The door groans again behind her as she pulls it shut, and the chain falls back into its hasp as if nothing had ever been touched.

At 14:00 on Day 3, Ves is back at the Bell Gate with the clapper wrapped in her cloak. The Pass Guard at the gate look at the bundle, then at her face, and then they look away. Nobody asks her what she is carrying. Ves goes up the last of the street without stopping, and she carries the clapper into the light of the town as if it weighs nothing at all.
