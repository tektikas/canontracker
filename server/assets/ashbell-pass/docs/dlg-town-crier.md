# Dialogue: The Town Crier

Scene: the lower market, beside the crier's stand at the Salt Gate. The barks run in order across Days 1 to 4. Speakers: HARL, the town crier, with passers-by and guards. This is Harl's own script, so each bark is one line.

HARL: Hear ye, hear ye! The Red Mule Caravan is back in Kettlehold, and its wagons are loaded with lamp oil and salt. Fair prices on the upper road for anyone who gets here early.

(A passer-by asks what lamp oil costs. Harl does not look up from his board.)

HARL: Four coppers the measure, at the Salt Gate and nowhere else. Anyone who tells you different has been drinking the cheap cider at the back of the Kettle & Coal.

(Day 1. A rider comes down Kiln Street with a grey mule on a lead. The crowd parts for her without being asked.)

HARL: Day One, friends, and the caravan scout is home. Ves Calloway has ridden through the Bell Gate after six years away. Make room, make room, and let the scout breathe.

(A boy with a tray of hot pies shouts that the scout owes him a pie. Harl ignores him.)

HARL: Curfew is at dusk, as it always is. When the bell rings, the Cinder Bridge closes for the night, and the Pass Guard will walk you home whether you like the walk or not. Keep your lamps lit on Kiln Street.

(Pell, the pot-boy from the inn, runs past with a crate of bread and calls the crier a long-winded old goat. Harl pretends not to hear.)

HARL: Day Two. There is talk at the foot of the Bell Gate about the bridge, and the talk is loud. I say mind your own business and buy your salt before the stalls close.

(A woman with a basket of eggs asks whether the market will be open tomorrow. Harl shrugs and keeps calling.)

HARL: Day Two, evening, and the bell has gone quiet. Hear ye, the bell is silent! Nobody in Kettlehold can tell me why it did not ring, and nobody has rung it since. Stay off the Cinder Bridge tonight.

(A guard named Sergeant Fennick, who has been on his feet since noon, asks Harl to stop shouting. Harl offers him a sip from his flask instead.)

HARL: Day Three. Captain Rourke swears he watched the tower all through the dark, and he has the look of a man who has not slept since. Odo Fenn swears he was on the bridge. Both cannot be right, friends, and I am not paid to guess which one is lying.

(A salt trader from the Foot of the Pass wants to know if the market is safe. Harl says the market has always been safe, which is not an answer.)

HARL: Day Three, and the Order has said nothing. Abbess Hesper sent word down the stair that the Ember Well is closed to visitors, except by her own word. Mind the Tallow Stair in the dark. It is steeper than it looks.

(Day 4. Harl has his script open on the board and reads it out with care, one note at a time.)

(Script note, Day 4: From 07:00 to 08:00 on Day 4, Mother Brisk is at the Salt Gate buying salt for the inn.)

HARL: Day Four. The bell rang at dawn for the first time in two days, and the one who rang it has not said a word since. Hear ye, be kind to the ringer, and do not ask for an answer.

(A child asks what the ringer is called. Harl says the town will learn it soon enough, and that is the end of the matter.)

HARL: Curfew at dusk, as always. Lamps on Kiln Street, and no one on the Cinder Bridge once the bell has rung. That is all the news I have, and it is all the news you will get from me today.

(Harl folds his board under one arm and walks off toward the Kettle & Coal. The crowd starts to thin, and the pies go cold on the tray.)
